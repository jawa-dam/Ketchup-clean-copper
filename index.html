Stage 1

The Discovery

How an accidental ketchup spill led to a surprising observation.

My Unexpected Observation

I was making a burger and fries. Before putting ketchup on my burger, I squeezed the first bit into my copper sink because I didn't want the watery ketchup to get on my bread or burger patty.

I accidentally left it there for about 20 minutes.

When I returned and rinsed the ketchup away, the spots where the ketchup had dripped looked clean and shiny. I had not wiped or scrubbed those spots.

That unexpected observation made me curious: could ordinary ketchup help remove tarnish from copper?

Ketchup contains acidic ingredients that may react with some tarnished copper surfaces, but this is based on a single accidental observation — not a controlled scientific study. Results may vary by material, finish, and ketchup brand.

Stage 3

The Ketchup Challenge

Follow these five steps to run your own ketchup-and-copper experiment.

0 / 5

  1. Gather Your Materials

    You need: a bottle of ketchup, a copper item (penny, jewelry, or small copper piece you own), paper towels, a camera or phone, and a timer.

  2. Document the Before

    Take a clear, well-lit photograph of the tarnished copper surface. Note the lighting conditions and camera angle so you can replicate them later.

  3. Apply Ketchup & Wait

    Apply a small amount of ketchup to a test area. Set a timer for 20 minutes. Record the ketchup brand and variety you used.

  4. Rinse & Observe

    When the timer goes off, rinse the area with water only — do not scrub. Observe carefully: did the surface change? Look the same? Something unexpected?

  5. Document the After

    Photograph the tested area under the same lighting and at the same angle as your before photo. Compare the two images side by side. Record your honest findings.

🏆 Experiment Complete!

You've followed the scientific method: observe, hypothesize, test, and record. Share your results with the community!

Safety Notice: This experiment uses ordinary household ketchup. Do not mix ketchup with cleaning chemicals. Test only on items you own and have confirmed are solid copper. Do not use on collectible coins, plated items, lacquered surfaces, or items with gemstones. Always check manufacturer care instructions first. Results may vary. This is an observation-based activity, not a scientifically proven cleaning method.
Stage 4

Y'all Too Science Lab

Discover the chemistry behind this surprising observation.

🧪

Acetic Acid (Vinegar)

Ketchup contains vinegar, which is acetic acid. This weak acid may dissolve copper oxide — the dark tarnish that forms on copper surfaces over time.

🍅

Citric Acid (Tomatoes)

Tomatoes are naturally acidic, containing citric acid. Working alongside the vinegar, this provides a second pathway that may help break down copper tarnish.

⚛

The Chemical Reaction

When these acids contact copper oxide (CuO), they can break it into soluble copper salts that rinse away with water — potentially revealing the cleaner copper underneath.
